Stage 1 · A Mystery

Two Balls, Two Surfaces

Veera rolls a ball with the exact same push — once on rough grass, once on smooth, icy tiles.

Watch closely — which ball do you think will travel farther?

Why did the ball on ice travel so much farther, even with the same push?

You found it! Rough surfaces grip and slow things down more than smooth ones. That invisible grip is called friction. Let's explore forces from the start!
Stage 2 · Discover

Push, Pull & Force

A push moves something away from you. A pull moves something toward you. Both are kinds of force. A force can make something:

And when two surfaces rub together, a force called friction pushes back against motion — it always opposes movement between the surfaces.

Friction isn't always bad — it's the reason your shoes grip the floor and your bicycle brakes work! Let's experiment with pushing forces first.
